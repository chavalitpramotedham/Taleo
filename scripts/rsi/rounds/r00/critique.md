**Page average: about 2.5 out of 5.**

## Whole set
- **Character consistency, 3.5.**
  - Ody, Hoot, Gran and Argos keep their look on every page, which is the strength of prepared parts.
  - Biscuit (the boat) changes on every page: star sail, dot sail, plank, card.
  - Polly never shows her scale.
- **Storytelling, 2.**
  - Each page reads as "characters standing in a row in front of a place".
  - The key object, which carries the plot, is missing on 9 of 18 pages.
  - Almost no physical interaction: no hugs, nothing handed over, nobody lifting or riding anything.
  - Ody stands on water on 6 pages.

## Failure modes, ranked by impact
1. **Required props are missing.** Feather (p10 target, p11), hat, sack, harps, bed, table, loom, tiller. The "main action's object" rule exists, but it doesn't hold.
2. **Vehicles and supports.** Characters at sea aren't in the boat, and the boat's design isn't the same from page to page. Characters stand on water or float on planks.
3. **Duplicated characters (p7) and a hidden hero (p8).**
4. **Scale by role.** The giant is drawn child-sized, and sizes jump between pages (Ody on p14, Argos on p16).
5. **See-through, piled figures.** Overlapping figures show each other's lines; the Larks, Argos and Sissy are a jumble.
6. **Weather against light.** It rains in a sunset (p15).
7. **No physical interactions.** Hugs, holding and lifting all read as standing side by side.

## Targets for the rounds
- Code guarantees:
  - one copy of each character;
  - the hero always visible;
  - a key prop drawn whenever the text needs it;
  - characters on a boat when the place is the sea;
  - a size floor for a giant;
  - occlusion done properly (opaque figures).
- Prompt work:
  - stage a prop at its holder's hand;
  - a group count that follows the text;
  - recurring props (the boat Biscuit) kept as a stamp across pages.
