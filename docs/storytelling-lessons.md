# Storytelling lessons

What the storyteller has been taught, and why - from tests on whole books
written through the real storyteller, with the same setups and choices,
judged blind against the previous version (clarity, coherence, appeal, and
every confusing moment listed).

## 1. Clarity comes first (15 Nov 2026)

Books for the youngest were confusing to read aloud: a "dino stone" nobody
had met, a star that suddenly talked, a friend left stuck in the mud and
then fine, a lost duck turning up held by a toy no one had mentioned, three
threads at once (a fallen tower, a dino hunt, a lost duck).

The cause was the instructions themselves. Every page was asked for a
giggle, a hook to the next page, a never-repeated new word to teach, a
fresh visible action, a refrain and a surprise - and nothing asked for one
problem told in order. On a page of 16 words the demands won and the story
lost: actions were picked to fit the page's word, surprises were random.

What changed:

- **Clear and sensible is now the first priority**, above engagement and
  the moral: one want, shown at the start; every page following from the
  last, with causes the child can see; introduced before used, and always
  called the same thing; characters who act and speak sensibly (never
  amused when a friend is upset); objects that only talk if the story made
  them magical; threads kept (stuck friends helped out, promises of help
  kept, teases paid on the next page); a simple map (characters get to new
  places on a page); humour that makes sense the moment it happens.
- **The youngest (under 3)** get their own rules: one goal, at most one new
  character and one new thing a segment, each page one small step said
  plainly, the same word for the same thing, central characters who do
  things.
- **The plot problem is the test of the lesson.** In a sharing story the
  problem is whether to share - an accident must never make the choice
  for the characters. (Dropping this line in an earlier draft let a gust of
  wind solve the sharing story, and older books got worse.)
- **A private plan**, written with segment 1 and handed back with every
  later one: want, problem, ending - three sentences. Planning the steps in
  between made older books contort themselves back onto the plan after each
  choice; the destination alone keeps them on one line.
- **Gentle themes get gentle wants** (say goodnight to the moon), not an
  invented mishap; nothing starts out lost or broken unless we see it
  happen.
- **Choices name only what the child has met**, and the next segment
  follows from whichever is chosen.
- **A typed idea is answered once.** The aside is read out before the page,
  so the page must not answer it again (this needed saying in the schema as
  well as the prompt - "show the consequence of the choice" was pulling the
  other way). An idea that fits is woven in as a way to tackle the one
  problem; one that doesn't is answered by the aside alone, never forced in.
  The model kept answering the idea in the page text whatever it was told,
  so the code now settles it: when the page's opening two sentences already
  name the idea (the rocket, the dinosaur - characters' names don't count),
  the separate aside is dropped and the idea is answered once.
- The word to teach is taken from what the page needs to happen anyway;
  a central character written out again as "new" is folded back into the
  one we have.

Results (clarity out of 10, old -> new, averaged over judgings): ages 1-2
lost item 5 -> 8, ages 2-3 big feeling 4 -> 8, ages 2-3 party 5 -> 6, ages
3-4 adventure 7 -> 7, ages 1-2 bedtime 8 -> 7.5, ages 4-5 mystery 7.5 -> 6,
ages 6+ sharing 6 -> 8. Mean 6.1 -> 7.2; the gains are largest at the ages
where the problem was.

Still to look at: mysteries (ages 4-5) - clue logic and repeated
confessions. Single books vary a lot, and so does the judge (one book
scored 5, 7 and 8 on three readings): compare several books per setup
before trusting a small difference.

Cost: about $0.10 a book with the storyteller, $0.01 a judgement.

## 2. The child's own ideas come first (15 Nov 2026)

A parent typed "summon the amazing dragon Vhagar ... and fight the Eye
with dragonfire" into a Lord-of-the-Rings-style story. The storyteller
answered with "Dragons? Anyone?" - only an owl hooted back. No dragon.
The instructions had said to weave in an idea only "if it fits", and
otherwise to answer it and carry on; with "nothing appears from nowhere"
on top, the safe move was to sidestep anything wild.

Now a typed idea that is safe and kind (or can be made so) really happens,
on the very next page: with a way in the child can see (a wish that works,
a bubble that glows), introduced plainly, and helping with the story's one
problem so the plot stays clear. Its spirit is kept but made gentle (the
dragon asked to fight lights the cold fire pit instead), anything borrowed
from a book, film or game gets a fresh name of the story's own, and only
an unsafe or unkind idea is declined - warmly, with something in the same
spirit. Pretending to try an idea and having it fail is ruled out.

Tested on five books (ages 2-6): the dragon, a unicorn, a rocket and a
dinosaur-dad all happened on the next page and carried the plot; "the
puppy bites the cat really hard" was turned to gentleness in the story.

## 3. Brief only what the painter can draw (15 Nov 2026)

Pictures are put together from whole characters in a set of poses, a
painted place and ready-drawn things, and laid out by code. A brief that
asks for anything else comes out wrong however well the rest is done. So
the storyteller now tells each moment in the painter's terms: the words
stay free, only the picture brief changes.

**How it was found (no new books).** Every page of the six bench books and
their 62 rounds (769 pages) was laid out again by today's layout code from
the composer's saved tags, and checked by script; then about 150 pages
were looked at by eye against their briefs: the latest round of each bench
book, the three reader books painted by today's painter (14-15 Nov), one
from the day before, and a whole book made on 15 Nov with live settings.
The 19 older library books (painted whole by the earlier painter) were
only counted, for what their briefs ask for, not judged on their pictures.
Each rule below comes from at least two books.

| What failed | Pages | E.g. |
|---|---|---|
| Poses that can't be drawn: lying, curled up, on a tummy, swimming, diving, sliding, upside down, spinning | ~14 | a dragon "curled up on the ledge" drawn standing; a penguin "swims" standing on the pond |
| Named things missing or misdrawn | ~14 | the fishing hole, the hook on the cairn, the mouse hole, the oars; a sledge drawn as a sailboat |
| Part of a character | ~11 | "the yak's head pokes through the tent flap" (the whole yak inside), "her face fills the picture", "jammed in the doorway up to her shoulders" |
| Sitting in a wagon, wheelbarrow, tail curl; on a bench or picnic blanket | ~11 | the baby "sits in the wagon" on four pages, standing behind it on every one |
| Standing on top of things | ~10 | a penguin on the table, on a flowerpot; a frog on the robot's head (floating) |
| Carried in hands, arms, on a hip, in a tail or trunk | ~7 | "carrying Kai and Ellie in his cupped hands": one on his chest, one in the sky |
| Touch between a giant and a tiny one, or by wings, tail, trunk, rope | ~6 | "her tail wrapped round them in a hug": Nell standing alone in front |
| More than four characters | ~4 | the tea party: the hero hidden behind the table, the giant octopus over the rest |
| A view out of a window, or inside and outside a tent at once | ~3 | a tent drawn inside the tent |

What works and is kept: riding on a shoulder, back or head (fixed in code
the same day), riding in a boat, sitting at a table or on a chair, log or
stone, being tucked into bed, hugs between two of a size, a giant at its
stated size, and anything from the piece library.

**The rules** (in "BRIEF ONLY WHAT THE PAINTER CAN DRAW", `writerPrompt`
in `src/server/prompts.ts`): whole characters; the poses that can be drawn,
and the nearest one for those that can't; carrying only as a ride (or in a
boat); seats and standing on the ground; touch between sizes; one moment
per picture; four characters at most; at most two named things a page,
the painter's ready pieces listed by name, anything else given a plain
shape and size; one place seen from inside it, water no deeper than the
knees. The old examples that asked for "tumbling head over heels" and
"peeking round a tree" are gone.

**Checked.** Four setups (three from the test books, one held out: a
birthday party, chosen for crowds and tables), written by the live
storyteller with the old and new instructions, the same choices, text
only: briefs asking for something hard went from 8 of 36 pages to 3, and
from 2.8 to 1.1 hard asks per 10 pages (`scripts/rsi/briefcheck.py`
counts them; on the existing books it finds 44-52% of briefs asking for
at least one). The stories read as well (read side by side), with one
cost: the storyteller sometimes changes the event to suit the picture (a
ride became holding hands), so the rule now says a ride is a fine picture.
Seven pages of the new books were painted: the party pages read at a
glance and the shoulder ride held; the one weak page had two moments in
one brief, hence the one-moment rule.

**Then given to the painter (16 Nov, painting lesson 19):** sitting in a
wagon, cart, wheelbarrow or on a sledge; standing or sitting on top of a
table, rock, stump, log, chest, box, flowerpot or cloud; and lying down
along the ground. The rules now allow them, and a second run of the four
setups used them (Moss on the stepping rocks, a hedgehog on a log, Biscuit
lying by Mia's feet) with hard asks still at 4 of 36 pages.

**Riding only on giants (16 Nov).** In a reader's book, a baby "on Mama's
shoulder" was drawn pasted to her chest: riding works only on a true giant.
The rule is now "no one on anyone of a size", in the words as well as the
picture: a baby rides in a pram or wagon, a small friend walks alongside,
and only a giant (at least three times the rider's height: a stone giant,
a troll, a dragon) is ridden. In the painter, a ride or a hold ("in Mama's
arms", "on his hip", "in Gran's lap") on anyone smaller than about two and
a half times the rider's size puts the rider at their side instead.

## 4. Pictures follow what's new, and never look the same twice (16 Nov 2026)

**What happened.** "Skiing down the tallest snow mountain" (16 Nov), page 3:
"Out tiptoed three round, shaggy ice monsters... Coliboo's blanket slipped
and flew away! Then, far above, a deep RUMBLE began." The monsters came out
small and half-hidden behind the family, and the family stood exactly as on
the page before (Coliboo in the middle, Mama and Dada either side - the
same line-up on all three pages of the part).

**Why.**
- The storyteller never declared the monsters: `newCharacters` was empty
  and the page's `characters` listed only the family. The painter places
  only listed characters, so the composer had to scribble the monsters in
  as loose shapes, at the edge.
- The brief put the newcomers "behind the pines on the left" - out of the
  way of the cast, instead of at the heart of the picture.
- The page held three new events (the monsters, the blanket, the
  avalanche), so no one of them owned the picture.
- Nothing asked for the picture to differ from the last one, so each brief
  restated the same arrangement.

**The rules now** (storyteller, picture briefs; composer):
- **Everyone who appears is a character** - in `newCharacters` where they
  first appear and in every page's `characters` they're on. A group that's
  always together is one character.
- **The new thing takes the stage** - on the page it first appears, a new
  character, thing or event is in the centre or foreground, big, whole and
  doing something; the familiar cast step aside, turned towards it,
  reacting.
- **One new event per page** - the next happening waits for the next page;
  where a page still holds more, the picture shows the biggest new one.
- **Every picture looks different from the last** - change at least two of:
  camera, who's in the centre and in front, the left-to-right order, poses,
  facing, the part of the place. Never the same line-up twice running.
- The composer gets the same two rules ("the newest thing is the star",
  "different from the last page"), checked against the previous page's
  brief it is already given.

The principle: **a composition must be relevant (about this page's new
action, thing or event), focused (the new thing is the star), and dynamic
(each page visibly changes from the last) - a tidy row of the same
characters is a failed picture.**

Where: `writerPrompt` (PICTURE BRIEFS) and `composerPrompt` in
`src/server/prompts.ts`.


## 5. The tap word, its doer, its motion and sound tell one action (16 Nov 2026)

**What happened.** "Skiing down the tallest snow mountain": page 1's word
was "gigantic" - never said on the page - with Coliboo growing; page 3's
"tiptoe" belonged to the ice monsters but Coliboo hopped. An audit of all
28 library books (403 pages) found 12 words not on their page, 12 targets
who weren't doing the word, 2 targets not in the picture, and dozens of
motions and sounds that contradicted their word ("asleep" floating off, a
"hop" that nods, a "tiptoed" that snores, a "wobble" that drips).

**The rules now** (all checked in code, on the server as a part is
written and in the reader for books written before):
- **The word is on the page** - a form of it is found ("crept" for creep,
  "bumblebee" for bee); one the page never says is replaced by the doing
  word of the target's own sentence, acted out its own way.
- **The target does the word** - read from the word's own clause: "Tilly
  poured, the mice danced" stays the mice; "whispered Kai" is Kai; "Out
  tiptoed three ice monsters" is the monsters; "he gave Bao a hug" stays
  the one who hugs.
- **The target is in the picture** - a character who acts out the word is
  added to the page's characters.
- **Motion and sound fit the word** - each kind of word has motions and
  sounds that act it out; a choice outside them is put right, a fitting
  one is kept.
- **One form everywhere** - the word lit in the text, the bubble over the
  picture and the voice all say the page's own form ("hopped").

Where: `src/lib/tap-word.ts`, `fitActing` in `src/lib/actions.ts`,
`cleanAction` in `src/server/claude.ts`, `tapTarget` in
`src/components/StoryReader.tsx`; the storyteller's rule in `writerPrompt`.

## 6. The action is shown happening - and the child's choice is seen (16 Nov) - P0

The reader chose "call their phoenix friend and get a lift… fly above the
mountain". The words did it ("Ember rose up, up, up, with everyone safe on
her back… landed softly on a high ridge"), but no picture did: the briefs
showed Ember landing with her wings folded, everyone standing on the snow
beside her. A child who can't read saw a family standing next to a bird.

Why: the storyteller had declared Ember "as tall as Dada", and its own rule
forbids anyone riding a character of about their size - so the one picture
that was the point of the choice couldn't be briefed, and it fell back to
the standing scene after it. Flying wasn't among the poses it was told the
painter can draw, a line warned off "wings spread wide", and the page held
two events (the flight and the landing), of which it picked the calm one.

Now:
- **A creature that carries them is a giant** from the moment it's
  declared (at least three times the tallest rider, a back wide enough for
  all) - so the ride can be drawn.
- **Flying is a drawable pose** for anything with wings or magic, with
  riders sitting on a flying giant's back.
- **Show the action happening, not before or after it**: the picture
  catches the key action mid-way (the swoop, the flight, mid-slope); a
  journey is shown on the way; a page that goes up and lands shows the
  flight. The test: could a child who can't read tell from the picture
  what the words say happened?
- **The child's choice is seen**: the part after a choice shows that very
  thing happening, in action, on its first page or two.
- The composer puts a flyer up in the sky with its riders' tags after it;
  the layout reads a whole list of riders ("with Dada, Mama and Coliboo
  sitting on her back") and takes "her back" to be the giant's that can
  carry them, not whoever was named last.

Where: `src/server/prompts.ts` (storyteller PICTURE BRIEFS, composer
FLYING), `perchedOf` in `src/server/claude.ts`.
