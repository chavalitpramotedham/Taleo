# Cheaper Claude painters beat every specialist

The most cost-efficient route for Taleo is to keep the current SVG painter design and change which Claude model paints each layer; no specialised SVG model offers higher quality at lower cost. Painting every character layer on Sonnet 5.5 and the scenery on Haiku 5.5 (taught with a cached library of finished example sceneries) cuts an estimated **62% of painting cost**, taking a 14-page story from about $1.37 to about $0.52 and a page from roughly 10 cents to under 4, with no change to the output format. Both swaps are environment-variable changes, but both carry a quality question that only a side-by-side test on real pages can settle. Before that, cache and retry fixes with no quality risk recover roughly 10-20%, and afterwards reuse techniques (stored heads, poses and scenery plates expanded on the server) push the total towards 75-85%. The one specialist that beats frontier models in blind votes, QuiverAI's Arrow 2, is priced at Opus rates, and raster-then-vectorise pipelines are cheap but break the layered-primitive format. Training Taleo's own painter on stored Claude pictures is prohibited without Anthropic's prior written authorisation, and it has no price advantage over Haiku 5.5 anyway. Every dollar figure here is an estimate: no API key or production usage data was available, most third-party prices came from search summaries, not opened pricing pages, and the app's own activity log would replace the token estimates within a day.

## A page costs about 10-14 cents, and output tokens are 75-90% of it

How far each number can be trusted comes first, because the notes rest on three different kinds of evidence. Facts about the pipeline were read from the repository at commit `36c6476`. Anthropic's prices were read from the live pricing page on 12 November 2026. Everything else is an estimate or a reported figure: token counts are derived from character counts and prompt element budgets, and almost every non-Anthropic price, leaderboard score and paper result comes from search-result summaries, because the research session could not open the primary pages.

The verified architecture matches the brief with three refinements. Stories are 9, 14 or 18 pages (Quest, Adventure, Odyssey) in parts of 3-4 pages ([ages.ts](../src/lib/ages.ts)). The first two pages of every part already go to Sonnet, so **Opus paints the characters on only 3 of 9, 6 of 14 and 8 of 18 pages** ([route.ts](../app/api/illustrate/route.ts), [tiers.ts](../src/lib/tiers.ts)). And the three painters (scenery on Sonnet, quick characters on Sonnet, main characters on Opus) never share a prompt cache ([claude.ts](../src/server/claude.ts)). Verified list prices per million tokens are $4 input / $20 output for Opus 5.5, $2 / $10 for Sonnet 5.5 and **$0.10 / $0.50 for Haiku 5.5**, with cache reads at 0.05x input on the two larger models ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing.md)).

The baseline has three estimates that disagree, and the report keeps all three in view. A bottom-up model at about 30 output tokens per SVG element gives 6 cents for a Sonnet-character page and 11 cents for an Opus-character page with warm caches, and **$0.86 / $1.37 / $1.77 per Quest / Adventure / Odyssey**. A second estimate, using an OpenAI tokenizer as a proxy on SVG already in the repo, measured about 45 tokens per element and arrives at 13 cents a page and $1.82 per Adventure. The developers' own committed figure is "about 14 US cents a painted page" ([ages.ts](../src/lib/ages.ts)). The true figure sits in the 10-14 cent range; the percentages below are more reliable than the dollars, and all are stated against the $1.37 Adventure unless noted.

| Where an Adventure's painting money goes (estimate) | Share |
|---|---|
| Visible SVG output | 67% |
| Cold prompt-cache writes | 17% |
| Hidden Opus thinking (billed as output) | 9% |
| Uncached input | 7% |
| *By painter:* Opus characters (6 pages) | 46% |
| *By painter:* Sonnet scenery (14 pages) | 31% |
| *By painter:* Sonnet characters (8 pages) | 23% |

Two conclusions follow. Trimming prompts or briefs moves the total by a few percent at most, so the savings are in output volume, output price per token and cache writes. And Opus, painting under half the pages, takes nearly half the money, partly because its thinking cannot be switched off and is billed at $20 per million ([claude.ts](../src/server/claude.ts)).

## The ranked options: model swaps first, reuse second, new notation last

The table ranks every option that keeps the output contract intact: inner SVG primitives in wash/paint/ink/detail groups, the fixed palette, one tap-target group, streaming paint-in and reference-matched characters. Savings are estimated shares of painting cost for a 14-page story; they overlap and do not add.

| Rank | Option | Est. saving | Quality risk | Build effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cache keep-alive or 1-hour TTL, pre-warming, keeping the finished layer when the other fails | 10-20% | None | Small |
| 2 | Characters on Sonnet 5.5 on every page (drop Opus) | ~32% | Medium | Config only |
| 3 | Scenery on Haiku 5.5 with cached example sceneries | ~28-30% | Medium, untested | Config plus example set |
| 4 | Options 2 and 3 together | ~62% | Sum of both | As above |
| 5 | Stored character heads spliced in by the server, body redrawn | 35-40% of character tokens | Low; likeness improves | Low-medium |
| 6 | Per-character pose cache, filled as pages are painted | ~47% of character tokens once warm | Medium (repetition) | Medium |
| 7 | One scenery "plate" per location per book | ~60-65% of scenery tokens | Low-medium (static backdrops) | Medium |
| 8 | Server-expanded shorthand: group defaults, palette names, stamps, procedural texture | 2-5% for defaults and palette names; 10-20% of scenery tokens for stamps and texture | Low | Low-medium |
| 9 | Full compact line notation in place of SVG | 24-29% of drawn tokens | Medium-high | Moderate |
| 10 | Haiku 5.5 for characters too | ~95% | High | Config plus eval |

**The no-risk fixes come first because they cost nothing in quality.** In a 3-page part there is exactly one Opus character request, so its roughly 7,200-token cached prefix is written at 1.25x and rarely read back unless the next part starts within five minutes. A keep-alive ping per open story, or a 1-hour cache lifetime, recovers an estimated 9-12% of story cost; the saving is zero if production caches already stay warm, which the activity log's cache-read and cache-write columns will show immediately. Failure handling is the other leak: when one layer fails, the other layer's paid work is discarded and both are repainted, and a slow Opus start that trips the 60-second clock produces a page costing about $0.16 against a normal $0.11 ([claude.ts](../src/server/claude.ts), [usePaintings.ts](../src/lib/usePaintings.ts)). The admin dashboard also prices Sonnet cache reads at $0.20 where the price list says $0.10, and has no 1-hour write rate, so it needs correcting before these levers are judged ([metrics.ts](../src/server/metrics.ts)).

**The model swaps are the biggest lever and need no code.** All painter models and effort levels are environment variables. The team's own side-by-side note says Sonnet painted "at about half the cost, with somewhat less careful characters", and that plain Haiku 5.5 scenery "came out plainer and less finished" ([claude.ts](../src/server/claude.ts)). The Haiku verdict was reached without examples, and "less finished" is the weakness that examples of finish address. Haiku makes examples nearly free: a 20,000-token cached prefix holding about seven complete sceneries costs two hundredths of a cent per request, against 2.7 cents for the Sonnet scenery output it replaces. The only hard limit is Haiku's price step above 100,000 prompt tokens, where rates rise 5x ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing.md)). Haiku scenery falls from about 3 cents to about 0.2 cents per page, and an Opus page falls from 11 cents to 6 on Sonnet. Haiku 5.5 has no server-side fallback, so a refusal or overload fails the layer; that makes the keep-the-other-layer fix a prerequisite.

**The swaps change which structural project is worth building.** Once scenery runs on Haiku, the character layer is roughly 95% of the remaining bill (my arithmetic from the notes' figures), so scenery plates save almost nothing in dollars and the next lever is character reuse. Storing each character's head at design time and letting the painter draw only the body saves an estimated 35-40% of character tokens and removes the commonest likeness failures, since colours, signature features and face style live mostly on the head. That takes the Adventure to roughly $0.35, about 75% below today. If Haiku scenery fails its test instead, the plate becomes the scenery lever: both painters already share a fixed stage (ground line, horizon, character zone), so a scenery painted once per location is geometrically valid under any later page's characters ([claude.ts](../src/server/claude.ts)). All of these share one piece of plumbing, a stream-safe expander inside `paintPage` that turns short tags into whitelisted SVG, so the client, sanitizer, storage and paint-in need no change.

**A private notation is the last resort, and one popular trick is worthless.** Proxy-tokenizer measurements on the repo's own art show path data is already about half of all tokens and each whole-number coordinate is one token, so relative coordinates and whitespace tricks save nothing. The cheap wins are bare `<g class="ink">` groups with server-supplied attributes (about 32 tokens each) and named palette colours (about 5% overall). A full line-per-shape notation measured 24-29% smaller, but models learned to draw in SVG, and a 2026 study of compact data formats found up to 18% token savings at about a 9-point accuracy cost ([Notation Matters](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29676)). Full part rigs with pose-only output would cut character tokens 75-90%, but the paper-doll stiffness contradicts the illustrator prompt's first priority of "character, action and life"; they belong last, if at all.

## No specialised SVG model is both better and cheaper

The direct answer to the owner's second question is no. **QuiverAI's Arrow 2 Telos tops the Design Arena SVG leaderboard** at roughly 1,530-1,610 Elo against about 1,330 for Claude Opus 5.5, but it is priced at $6 input / $30 output per million tokens, with standard Arrow 2 at $4 / $20, exactly Opus 5.5's rate ([Design Arena](https://www.designarena.ai/leaderboard/svgs), [Vercel AI Gateway](https://vercel.com/ai-gateway/models/arrow-2)). Its lead is reported on logos, stickers, faces, animals and clipart, not multi-character scenes with layer conventions, and nobody has shown it re-posing a character from an SVG reference. It is a possible later quality experiment, not a saving.

Recraft's vector models cost a flat $0.08 per SVG ($0.30 for Pro), rank about 29th-30th on the same board, return a whole traced-style file with no layer classes, no tap target and no streaming, and take style references but not exact character identity ([Recraft pricing](https://www.recraft.ai/docs/api-reference/pricing)). The open research models are further away still. OmniSVG, the most relevant, needs 83-98 seconds per 4,096 tokens, was trained on a non-commercial dataset and targets icons and single objects ([OmniSVG](https://github.com/OmniSVG/OmniSVG)). StarVector's own documentation says it "will not work for natural images or illustrations" ([StarVector](https://starvector.github.io/starvector/)).

### Other providers' general models offer one credible fallback

Among general LLMs, the only sub-Sonnet-priced model near the top SVG cluster is **Gemini 3.8 Flash at about 1,293 Elo**, with reported output at $3.75 per million through 31 December 2026 and apparently doubling on 1 January 2027 ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing), [modelgrep](https://modelgrep.com/best/svg/google)). At Taleo's estimated scenery size that is 1.0-1.5 cents a layer against Sonnet's 2.7-4.0 (my arithmetic); after the reported increase the saving shrinks to about 25%. It is the fallback scenery painter if Haiku with examples fails, with two cautions: Gemini's safety filters can block benign output without a configurable threshold, and its prices change on fixed dates. The very cheap models (GPT-6 Luna at a reported $0.10 / $0.50, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) sit 100-130 Elo below the Opus cluster where they are ranked at all; Luna is priced identically to Haiku 5.5, so it adds a second vendor for no saving. DeepSeek's first-party API is governed by PRC law with no published retention guarantees, which rules it out for a children's product. None of this evidence tests Taleo's actual task, so leaderboard rank is a shortlist filter only.

### Raster-then-vectorise is cheap but is a different product

Generating a raster image and tracing it costs a reported $0.016-0.045 per picture, with fast open-weight image models at about $0.005-0.015 per megapixel and tracing free ([fal FLUX.2 klein](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/flux-2/klein/9b/base/llms.txt), [VTracer](https://github.com/visioncortex/vtracer)). That is no cheaper than the Sonnet-plus-Haiku configuration at under 4 cents, and it fails the plug-and-play test on every point. No tool emits wash/paint/ink/detail groups, so they must be rebuilt by heuristics. The tap target needs a separate segmentation call. A local tracing test produced 22-70 KB files of anonymous paths where the app stores compact primitives. And a recurring character is re-traced from a slightly different raster on every page. It would be a rebuild of the picture pipeline, justified only if the owner later wants a different look.

## Training Taleo's own painter is blocked by terms and beaten on price by Haiku

The terms constraint is unambiguous on the texts read directly. Anthropic's Usage Policy prohibits customers to "use outputs to train an AI model (e.g., 'model scraping' or 'model distillation') without prior authorization from Anthropic", with no "competing" qualifier ([Anthropic Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)). Its help article lists "Using Outputs as training targets for models" among prohibited uses and names only analysis-type models (classifiers, summarisers, search) as permitted ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12326764-can-i-use-my-outputs-to-train-an-ai-model)). The Commercial Terms separately bar using the services "to train competing AI models" ([Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)). **Taleo's stored paintings cannot be used as training targets for any model, open-weights or another vendor's, without written permission**, and that covers the existing activity log. Fine-tuning Claude itself is not an escape route: the only Claude model reported tunable on Bedrock was Claude 3 Haiku, reported retired in September 2026. Whether art briefs written by Claude count as outputs, and whether using stored paintings as in-context examples for another vendor's model is acceptable, are questions to put to Anthropic in the same letter. This is not legal advice.

The economics remove most of the motive. At the fine-tuning researcher's assumed 6,000 input and 12,000 output tokens per picture, a tuned Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite costs about 2.9 cents, while untuned Haiku 5.5 costs about 0.7 cents at verified list prices (my arithmetic). Only custom Amazon Nova models (a reported 0.2-0.3 cents) and small open-weight adapters on serverless hosts undercut Haiku, and no public evidence shows a small model distilled for one house style matching a frontier model on 150-300-element scenes. The published specialist models were trained on 2-5 million SVGs ([OmniSVG](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06263v3)). Self-hosting is worse at Taleo's stage: one long generation occupies a rented GPU for minutes, an estimated 7-13 cents per picture without steady concurrent load. Platform churn is also concrete, with OpenAI fine-tuning reported closed to new users.

## The staged plan: measure, fix, test two swaps, then build reuse

**Stage 0, this week, no quality risk.** Pull one week of the activity log to replace every estimate above: real tokens per layer, cache-read against cache-write ratios, Opus thinking (output tokens minus streamed SVG characters divided by about 2.7), and the slow, failed and reopened counters. Correct the prices in `metrics.ts`. Ship the cache keep-alive or 1-hour lifetime, pre-warm the three caches when the reader makes a choice, release reopen bursts one request per painter first, keep the finished layer when the other fails, and fall back in-process when Opus is slow. Send Anthropic the written authorisation request now, since its timing is unpredictable. Expected saving: 10-20%.

**Stage 1, the following one to two weeks, configuration experiments.** Build a fixed evaluation set of 20-30 real pages that includes the hardest cases (three characters, an action, a custom character) and judge blind, side by side. Test Sonnet 5.5 for all character pages, then Sonnet at low effort where it runs at medium today. Test Haiku 5.5 scenery with 5-10 varied example sceneries in the cached prefix, then at high effort, then with explicit element minimums. If Haiku fails, test Gemini 3.8 Flash for scenery. Change one lever at a time. Expected saving if both swaps pass: about 62%, to roughly $0.52 per Adventure and under 4 cents a page.

**Stage 2, the following month, engineering.** Build the stream expander with group defaults, palette names, stamps and procedural texture. Add head reuse next, then a pose cache saved on the family's characters so later books benefit. Add location, light and camera fields from the writer and per-book scenery plates if scenery is still on a paid-for model, or for continuity alone if it is on Haiku. Expected cumulative saving: 75-85%.

**Stage 3, only with evidence.** Trial a full compact notation against the evaluation set. Consider Haiku for characters once the character job has shrunk to placing a head and drawing a body. Revisit a tuned model only with written permission or a training set with clean rights, on per-token serverless hosting.

**Avoid.** The Batch API's 50% discount cannot serve pages a reader reaches in one to three minutes, because it carries no latency guarantee; it suits offline work such as generating the example library ([Anthropic batch docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/batch-processing.md)). Fast mode doubles the price. Structured JSON output for paintings adds roughly 10% in escaping. Arrow 2, Recraft Vector and the open research models do not deliver the cost goal. Raster pipelines, self-hosted GPUs, DeepSeek's first-party API and any training on stored Claude outputs without permission are all ruled out for the reasons above.

## Conclusion

The research went looking for a cheaper painter and found that the price collapse has already happened inside the vendor Taleo uses: Haiku 5.5's output costs one fortieth of Opus 5.5's, which makes worked examples, higher effort and even second attempts effectively free on the small model. Taleo's cost problem is therefore a quality-transfer problem: how much of the Opus and Sonnet finish can be taught to cheaper painters through examples, and how much drawing can be avoided by reusing shapes the app has already paid for. Both are testable in weeks with the existing code, format and vendor.

Three unknowns could reorder this plan, and all are cheap to resolve. The activity log will show whether the baseline is nearer 10 or 14 cents and how much Opus thinks. The evaluation set will show whether Sonnet characters and example-taught Haiku scenery are acceptable to the owner's eye, which no leaderboard predicts. And Anthropic's reply will decide whether a house-trained painter is ever an option. Until the first two are in hand, treat every saving in this report as a ranked estimate, not a forecast.
